Chapter 15 — The Errand of the People's Militia

Cassidy Darkstar · October 9, 2026

Cassidy Darkstar took up his road again at Demont's Place, in the land of Westfall. He came there alone, a druid of the Night Elves who was not yet much known in the wide world, and he carried an errand that was already his own. It was named The People's Militia, and it asked of him one plain thing: that the Defias, who had made that country their own, be thinned by his hand. Nothing was written of what he thought on arriving, and nothing need be. He had come to finish a thing, and he set about it.

The Defias Pathstalkers came first. One after another they fell before him, until five were overcome and that part of the errand was complete. It was not a great deed as the songs measure such things, but it was a true one and fully done, and Cassidy did not turn aside from it. Then he went after the Defias Knuckledusters, and these too he overcame, five of them, until the second count was full. So both tasks of The People's Militia were brought to their end, each in its turn, as a careful traveller finishes one stretch of road before taking the next.

In so short a stretch of the long road he gained no new strength, and he found nothing of great worth that might be named. He was neither felled nor hindered, and he walked with no companion. Yet the errand had been carried through by his own hand alone, and that is no small thing. Many such small labours are done in Westfall by those who pass through it, and few are remembered, but they are the stuff of which longer tales are made. A druid who sees a task through to its end, without complaint and without being asked twice, has already learned something the great ones also had to learn.

When the work was done he went on, south and west of Demont's Place, and came into The Dagger Hills, which he had not walked before. There his account falls silent for a while. The errand of the Militia was fulfilled but not yet reported, and the road beyond the hills ran on as roads do, past what he could see, toward whatever lay waiting at its next turning.
